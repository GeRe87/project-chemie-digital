index legend. Long legend terms/descriptions wrap; browser checks reject both
overlap and a legend clipped below its visible region. This fixes actual readability,

### Five additional boxplot interpretation slides

Each slide contains one concrete boxplot and its observations, with an explanation
of what can and cannot be concluded:

1. **Extreme observations:** 50 river values, including 90 mg/L; upper fence 37.25,
   upper whisker 24.5. A statistical flag is not an error diagnosis or exclusion rule.
2. **Rough symmetry:** 100 normal-like synthetic responses; median near box centre
   and comparable halves do not constitute a normality test.
3. **Right tail:** 100 skewed responses; unequal box halves/tails and multiple flags
   can reflect a genuine skewed pattern, not failed measurements.
4. **Two clusters:** 100 bimodal responses; median in the gap, which is invisible
   from the box alone. The accompanying raw points reveal it.
5. **Nearly uniform occupancy:** 100 evenly spaced responses; a symmetric box is
   not evidence for a bell shape.

The four 100-point shape sets reuse the accepted opening data, with their common
empirical mean 50 and sample SD 10 a.u. No new population-model claims are made.

### Sixteen histogram slides

- Definition: adjacent numerical bins; **initially equal widths**; counts as heights.
- Reading: centre/spread/occupancy, right tail and two occupied regions, one example
  per slide. Histograms do not recover time order or diagnose causes.
- 2D introduction and interpretation: **60 paired observations**, concentration and
  response; equal rectangular cells, numerical counts plus color; not a fitted
  calibration or evidence of causality.
- Same-data comparisons: one overly wide bin, 400 overly fine bins, ten useful bins,
  and a shifted origin. No observation changes or disappears between these views.
- Freedman–Diaconis, Scott and Sturges as starting rules, not universal optima;
  distinction between width/count, convenient-edge rounding, IQR=0, ties and
  measurement resolution. A 100-point numerical example checks the proposed widths.
- Unequal-width bins: why relative-frequency **density** is needed; construction
  from min/quartiles/max; dense-bulk/sparse-tail use and limitations. Each of the
  four adaptive bins contains 25 of 100 observations, but has a different width and
  height; each area is 25%, total area one. Repeated quantile edges must be merged.
- Executable ggplot2 lab using exactly the same 100 bimodal observations. Width 4
  shows the two groups; changing width to 40 hides them in one bar. Counts sum to
  100 in both runs.

Histograms declare all numeric edges and interval closure. The last bin includes
the upper endpoint; outside values, duplicate edges and unequal-width count heights
fail closed. See [ADR-0017](adr/0017-histogram-scene-primitive.md) for the additive
SceneDocument 1.10 contract and numerical/static-accessibility boundary.
